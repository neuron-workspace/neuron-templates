# Atomic notes

One idea per note, titled as a claim rather than a topic.

"Caching" is a topic and will grow into a dumping ground. "A cache is a bet that
reads outnumber writes" is a claim — it can be linked to, argued with, and
refined, and you know immediately whether a new paragraph belongs in it.

## Why it matters for linking

A note about one thing can be linked from anywhere that thing comes up. A note
about six things can only be linked vaguely, so it accumulates inbound links
that all mean something different. That is how a knowledge base becomes a pile:
not too many notes, but notes too big to point at precisely.

This is the mechanism behind [[Linking over filing]] — atomic notes are what
make links worth more than folders.

## The test

If you cannot write the title as a sentence someone could disagree with, the
note is probably a topic and wants splitting.

## When to break the rule

Reference material. A note listing every keyboard shortcut is not one idea and
should not pretend to be. See [[How to take a note]] for the distinction between
notes that think and notes that remember.
