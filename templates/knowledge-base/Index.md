# Index

A map of content: a note whose job is to point at other notes. It is the one
place you deliberately maintain by hand, because everything else here is found
by following links.

## Threads

- [[How to take a note]] — what goes in one, and what does not
- [[Atomic notes]] — why one idea per file
- [[Linking over filing]] — the argument against folder trees
- [[Maps of content]] — what this note is, and when to make another
- [[Working with the graph]] — reading the shape of what you have written

## How this grows

You do not plan a knowledge base. You write [[Atomic notes]], link them as you
go, and periodically notice that six notes are circling one idea — that is when
a new map of content earns its place. See [[Maps of content]].

Open the graph and this note is the obvious hub. That is not decoration: a hub
in the graph is a claim that these notes belong together, and when the graph
disagrees with your filing, the graph is usually right.
