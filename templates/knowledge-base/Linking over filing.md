# Linking over filing

A folder puts a note in one place. A link puts it in every conversation it
belongs to.

## The failure mode of folders

You write a note about hiring contractors for a project that is over budget.
Does it go in `hiring/`, `projects/`, or `finance/`? Whichever you pick, the
other two lose it. You will not remember which you chose, and six months later
you will write the note again.

Folders force a decision about *where something lives* at the exact moment you
know least about it.

## What links do instead

Link the note from every note it relates to. It is reachable from all of them,
and the connection survives: delete the link and the backlink goes with it,
because there was never a second copy of that fact to go stale.

This only works if the notes are small enough to point at precisely — see
[[Atomic notes]].

## Folders still earn their place

For *kinds* of thing, not topics. `daily/` for journal entries and
`attachments/` for files are fine, because those are formats, and a format
genuinely does belong in one place.

## Where this leads

Enough links and you get a structure nobody designed — which is what
[[Working with the graph]] is for reading, and [[Maps of content]] is for
navigating deliberately.
