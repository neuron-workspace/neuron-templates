# Maps of content

A map of content is a note whose content is links. [[Index]] is one.

## Why they exist

[[Linking over filing]] leaves you with a web and no front door. Search finds a
note when you know what you are looking for; a map of content is for when you do
not — when you want to know *what is here* about a subject.

## When to make one

Not in advance. A map earns its place when you notice several notes circling the
same idea and you keep having to remember which ones. That is the signal: the
grouping already exists in your head and is not written down anywhere.

Making maps first is just folders with extra steps — you are back to deciding
structure before you have the material.

## What goes in one

Links, and one line each saying why you would follow them. A bare list of titles
is worse than the sidebar, which at least sorts alphabetically without you.

## They are notes

A map can be linked to, appear in the graph, carry tags, and be wrong. If a map
stops matching what you have written, edit it — or delete it and let the links
carry the structure until a better grouping shows up in
[[Working with the graph]].
